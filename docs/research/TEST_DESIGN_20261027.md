# Pool regions, fix the likelihood, re-anchor depth

The next round of brms fits should not add more regional flexibility. Your date-blocked result, where the half-shift model `s(depth − 0.5·MLD, by = Region)` beat no-MLD in all 8 unseen-date comparisons while the regional depth-varying MLD models won only on seen dates, is what validation theory predicts: region-specific terms win conditional (seen-cluster) tests and fail marginal (new-cluster) ones. The highest-value tests come in four steps. **First, make every fit about 5× cheaper and statistically honest.** Bin profiles to 5 m, move to a log-ratio likelihood that cannot predict chl_norm < −1, let σ vary with depth, and collapse the cruise-confounded ENSO intercept into a single date effect. **Second, put MLD into a partially pooled (hierarchical GAM) structure.** This means a global depth-varying MLD effect plus shrunken regional deviations, in place of 72-81 h regional tensors. **Third, test ecologically better depth anchors than MLD**: pycnocline/thermocline depth (z(N²max), z20, isopycnals), and a parametric Gaussian-peak model that separates "MLD shifts the DCM" from "MLD erodes the DCM". **Fourth, test the two measurement artefacts the ratio response is exposed to**: daytime fluorescence quenching in the 5-15 m denominator, and small-denominator inflation in the Central Red Sea. The matrix at the end has **10 core fits plus 4 conditional or optional ones**. On binned data most fall in a roughly 4-16 h class, so the core set costs about what 2-3 current fits do. Four tests (thermocline coordinate, quenching, raw-Chl denominator, Red Sea salinity tracer) need variables that are probably not in the current bundle: temperature/salinity profiles, cast time with position, and raw Chl with raw surface Chl. The primary criterion for every test should be date-grouped held-out performance (joint log score plus RMSE, paired by date and bootstrapped over dates). Cast-holdout results should be reported but never used to choose structure.

## Your two validation cohorts answer different questions, and the unseen-date one is the target

The split between your cohorts is not a contradiction. Holding out casts from dates that stay in training is a *conditional* test: the date intercept is learned from sibling casts, so the model only has to interpolate within a known cruise-day. Holding out whole dates is a *marginal*, leave-one-cluster-out test: the date effect must be integrated out. Merkle and co-authors formalize this split: marginal information criteria correspond to leave-one-cluster-out CV, and conditional ones to leave-one-unit-out ([Merkle, Furr & Rabe-Hesketh 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.04452v2)). Vehtari's CV FAQ makes the same point practically: pointwise LOO "is valid if the focus is in the conditional data (observation) model", and the partitioning should match the actual prediction task ([CV FAQ](https://users.aalto.fi/~ave/CV-FAQ.html)). Roberts et al. show in simulation that random or within-structure splits leave dependent data on both sides, give "too optimistic" error, and make "model selection … favour too complex models". Blocking by group fixes this, and blocks should be at least as large as the dependence range ([Roberts et al. 2017, Ecography](https://www.biometrie.uni-freiburg.de/mitarbeiter/dormann/roberts-et-al-2017-ecography.pdf)). The regional MLD models' seen-date advantage (validation RMSE 1.401-1.409 vs 1.540 without MLD) is therefore partly borrowed from date-level information. Their failure on unseen dates is the more trustworthy signal for any use that generalizes to new cruises or a climatology.

Two features of the data make this worse. First, **adjacent-depth residual correlation is about 0.97**. Under an AR(1) approximation, a 141-row cast then carries roughly n(1−ρ)/(1+ρ) ≈ 2 independent residuals. The 152k rows therefore hold information closer to a few thousand independent values than to 152k. This is an inference from standard AR(1) algebra. It implies that current posterior intervals are too narrow and that smoothing parameters are pushed toward wiggliness, because "a wiggly trend and an autocorrelation function with large ϕ are two ways to describe the same pattern" ([Simpson 2018](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/322248v2.full)). Second, **ENSO labels are close to a function of cruise**. A held-out date whose cruise contributes other dates to training can still borrow that cruise's level through the `(1|Ocean:Region:enso)` intercept, a quiet leak in the date-blocked test. For the unseen-date target, what matters is that the *total* between-date variance is estimated well. How it is split between date and ENSO is prior-driven when the two are confounded, and Gelman warns that group-level variances with fewer than about 5 levels are poorly identified without an informative prior ([Gelman 2006](https://econwpa.ub.uni-muenchen.de/econ-wp/em/papers/0404/0404001.pdf)). Ecologically, ENSO is also a poor *pooled* effect. La Niña shoals the thermocline at the equatorial line islands (the 25 °C isotherm near Jarvis sat at about 13 m in March 2000 against about 97 m in March 2002) ([Gove 2005](https://data.nodc.noaa.gov/coris/library/NOAA/CRCP/project/1221/upwelling_variable_jarvis_island_gove_thesis.pdf)). In model results for 1997, El Niño deepened the central-equatorial nutricline but *raised* the western-basin nitracline ([Radenac et al. 2005](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00000/938/590.pdf)). The Red Sea link to ENSO runs through *winter* surface chlorophyll (r = 0.71 with MEI), not the autumn DCM ([Raitsos et al. 2015](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/83431/1/Raitsos_2015_GRL.pdf)). A single Sep-Oct 2011 cruise cannot identify ENSO there in any case.

The decision rule follows from the same sources. Vehtari treats |Δelpd| < 4 as small and warns that standard errors are underestimated when models are similar or misspecified. He advises choosing "a smaller model with similar predictive performance" ([CV FAQ](https://users.aalto.fi/~ave/CV-FAQ.html)). With 152k correlated rows, observation-level SEs are meaningless. Differences should be aggregated to the independent unit (date, or cruise where a cruise spans regions) and bootstrapped over those units.

## Gaussian chl_norm on 1-m rows costs compute and misstates uncertainty

The cheapest gains do not involve new science at all. A Gaussian on chl_norm = Chl/surface − 1 puts **6-7% of predictive draws below −1**, a physically impossible negative chlorophyll. Fitting `log(Chl/surface) = log1p(chl_norm)` fixes this by construction. It is exactly equivalent to brms `lognormal()` on Chl with `offset(log(surface))`, where σ is "the residual standard deviation on the log-scale" ([brms families vignette](https://paulbuerkner.com/brms/articles/brms_families.html)). The alternative is `Gamma(link = "log")` with the same offset, whose μ is the mean ratio and which defaults to a log link in brms ([brmsfamily reference](https://paulbuerkner.com/brms/reference/brmsfamily.html)). A skew-normal on chl_norm keeps the original scale but still allows values below −1, so it is a poor first choice. Three practical cautions apply. First, log-scale elpd is not comparable with the current chl_norm-scale fits until each pointwise log-likelihood gets the Jacobian term −log1p(chl_norm_i) added. Second, back-transformed *mean* enhancement is exp(η + σ²/2) − 1 and the median is exp(η) − 1, so RMSE on the chl_norm scale must use the mean. Third, casts with Chl ≤ 0 need a floor at a sensor-specific dark value, or left-censoring with `cens()`, before the log is defined. These are derivations from the notes, not cited results.

Variance is not constant along the profile. Variability concentrates around the DCM, and the Central Red Sea has a heavy right tail. brms distributional regression lets σ take any predictor, including smooths (`sigma ~ s(x0) + (1|fac)`) ([brms distreg vignette](https://paulbuerkner.com/brms/articles/brms_distreg.html)). A `student()` family offers "robust … regression … less influenced by outliers" ([brmsfamily reference](https://paulbuerkner.com/brms/reference/brmsfamily.html)). These additions are cheap next to the 152k-row likelihood.

**Binning to 5 m** is the single largest compute lever. It cuts rows from about 152k to about 30k and reduces lag-1 residual correlation from 0.97 to about 0.86 under AR(1). The DCM stays resolved, since published peak widths run from about 4 m in eutrophic water to about 75 m in ultra-oligotrophic water, with tens of metres typical offshore ([Mignot et al. 2011](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/8/2391/2011/bg-8-2391-2011.pdf)). Averaging chl_norm within a bin equals averaging Chl and then dividing, because the surface denominator is constant per cast. The roughly 5× runtime reduction is an inference that assumes the likelihood dominates cost, so verify it with the first fit. For the residual dependence that remains, brms `ar(time, gr, p = 1, cov = FALSE)` is the cheap regression formulation for Gaussian-type families ([brms ar() reference](https://paulbuerkner.com/brms/reference/ar.html)). Bürkner reported about a 50% runtime overhead on a panel example ([Stan Discourse 10118](https://discourse.mc-stan.org/t/ar-model-in-brms-how-to-speed-it-up-and-for-unbalanced-panel-structure/10118)). The covariance (`cov = TRUE`) route on a GAM failed to finish in a week on 24 cores ([Stan Discourse 11965](https://discourse.mc-stan.org/t/computational-time-of-a-fairly-complex-gam-with-arma-structure-in-brms/11965)). Within-chain threading helps less for cheap Gaussian likelihoods. The vignette's Poisson example gained about 1.8× on 2-4 cores, and rows should be shuffled for load balance ([brms threading vignette](https://paulbuerkner.com/brms/articles/brms_threading.html)), except in `ar()` models, which need cast/depth order.

## Partial pooling, not regional tensors, is the principled home for MLD

In Pedersen et al.'s hierarchical GAM taxonomy, `s(depth_s, by = Region)` with no global smooth is **model I**. Each region has its own curve and its own penalty, and no information about curve *shape* is shared. "There is no way for the model to share information on function shape between grouping levels without the global smoother", and only models with a global term can predict an unobserved group ([Pedersen et al. 2019, PeerJ](https://peerj.com/articles/6876)). Model **GI** adds a global smooth plus `by = Region` deviations with a first-derivative penalty (`m = 1`), which limits the concurvity Pedersen et al. warn about. Model **GS** gives the deviations one shared smoothing parameter through `bs = "fs"`. The authors say the choice is scientific, warn against selecting purely on AIC, and recommend held-out prediction. brms supports `fs` and `t2` but not `te()` ([Pedersen et al. 2019](https://peerj.com/articles/6876)). With only two Oceans, Ocean belongs in the model as a fixed `by =` curve, not a random smooth, given Gelman's few-groups result ([Gelman 2006](https://econwpa.ub.uni-muenchen.de/econ-wp/em/papers/0404/0404001.pdf)).

The same logic explains the tensor failure. `t2(depth, MLD, by = Region)` asks each region, some with few dates, to estimate a full two-dimensional surface with its own penalties. Pedersen et al. also note that tensor-product group terms can drop basis functions unevenly when there are few basis functions or few groups ([Pedersen et al. 2019](https://peerj.com/articles/6876)). The theory-preferred replacement is an *encompassing* model. It keeps the half-shift, adds one **global depth-varying MLD coefficient** `s(depth_s, by = mld_z)`, and adds **regional MLD deviations penalized toward zero** with tight priors on their `sds`. If the regional deviations matter on unseen dates, they survive the shrinkage. If not, the model collapses toward shift plus global MLD without a separate selection step. In brms the spline wiggliness lives in `sds` (default `student_t(3, 0, 2.5)`, the same as group-level SDs) ([brms set_prior](https://rdrr.io/cran/brms/man/set_prior.html)). The null-space part is a fixed effect with a flat default, so it needs its own proper prior ([Stan Discourse, G. Simpson](https://discourse.mc-stan.org/t/better-priors-non-flat-for-gams-brms/23012)). Penalised-complexity priors (an exponential on the SD, shrinking toward a zero-variance base model) are the principled default for "shrink toward simpler" components ([Simpson et al. 2017](https://projecteuclid.org:443/euclid.ss/1491465621)). Use `exponential(4)` or tighter on deviation `sds`, and check the implied wiggliness with `sample_prior = "only"`.

Log distance from shore deserves the same pooled, depth-varying treatment if it currently enters as a single depth-constant term. Across 35 NOAA-surveyed islands, 91% showed a significant island-mass effect, with nearshore enhancement up to 85.6%. Depth-integrated chlorophyll increased toward shore at 73% of surveyed islands, and the enhancement "occurs over the full euphotic depth range". The effect was strongest at the Northwestern Hawaiian Islands atolls and only 2.6% at Howland/Baker/Jarvis, where equatorial upwelling already raises the background ([Gove et al. 2016, Nat. Commun.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4757766)). Upward isopycnal motion near islands can also passively lift a DCM ([De Falco et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.894860/pdf)). Distance can therefore change the *ratio* in either direction: shoreward uplift plus higher surface Chl can lower relative enhancement while absolute biomass rises. A depth-varying distance smooth with Region-varying slopes tests this directly.

Before any brms fit, prescreen structure cheaply. brms builds smooths with mgcv's `smoothCon`. REML is an empirical-Bayes estimate of the same penalized model, and Pedersen et al. report `bam()` "can be upward of an order of magnitude faster" with many groups ([Pedersen et al. 2019](https://peerj.com/articles/6876)). Exact k is "not generally critical" once the EDF sits well below k − 1 ([mgcv choose.k](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/mgcv/html/choose.k.html)). The notes found no source calling this prescreen-then-refit workflow standard practice. It is a pragmatic step justified by the shared basis construction.

## Ecology anchors the DCM to light and the nutricline, so the half-shift is a proxy

The literature gives no coefficient of the form "DCM depth = a + b·MLD". The best-supported controls on DCM depth are light and nutrients. In the tropical Atlantic the chlorophyll maximum sat "statistically the same as the top of the nitracline" (slope 0.95, r = 0.95, n = 126) ([Cullen 2015](https://ftp.soest.hawaii.edu/dkarl/misc/dave/Oxford/09-Plankton%20Growth%20and%20Metabolism%20in%20the%20Epipelagic%20Zone/2015AnnuRevMarSci7-207-239-Cullen.pdf)). Oligotrophic DCMs sit at about 0.92-0.97 of the euphotic depth Zeu, rising to about 0.38 Zeu in eutrophic classes ([Uitz et al. 2006](https://people.mio.osupytheas.fr/~petrenko/TEACHING/OPCB343/Uitz_et_al._JGR_06_chloro_profiles_space.pdf)). Global BGC-Argo analysis concludes that "DCM appearance and depth are mainly controlled by light attenuation in the upper layer". There, MLD matters mainly as a *switch*: a DCM needs mixed-layer daily PAR above about 15 E m⁻² d⁻¹ ([Cornec et al. 2021](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00685/79669/82442.pdf)). Uitz et al. use the equivalent rule that profiles are stratified (Gaussian DCM) when Zeu/MLD > 1 and mixed otherwise ([Uitz et al. 2006](https://people.mio.osupytheas.fr/~petrenko/TEACHING/OPCB343/Uitz_et_al._JGR_06_chloro_profiles_space.pdf)). At Station ALOHA the 1% light depth moves from about 105 m in winter to 121 m in summer, and the DCM layer deepens with the isolumes ([Letelier et al. 2004](https://core.ac.uk/works/153904444)). In the Mediterranean, the subsurface maximum tracked the nitracline to within 20 m in the west but sat about 50 m above it in the east. The authors recommend judging MLD relative to the nitracline, not alone ([Barbieux et al. 2019](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/16/1321/2019/bg-16-1321-2019.html)).

These results suggest why a *half* shift works (an inference to be tested). MLD is a noisy correlate of thermocline and nutricline depth, so a partial shift captures the shared signal without pretending the DCM rides on the mixed-layer base. A temperature-criterion MLD can also overstate the actively mixing layer: in Southern Ocean night profiles more than 70% of winter fluorescence maxima lay *inside* the MLD, and a finer 0.005 kg m⁻³ density threshold tracked active mixing better ([Carranza et al. 2018](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10082652)). The ecologically sharper test re-expresses depth relative to a cast-level pycnocline or thermocline. Options are z(N²max) everywhere, z20 or z25 in the Pacific (25 °C was the Jarvis thermocline metric) ([Gove 2005](https://data.nodc.noaa.gov/coris/library/NOAA/CRCP/project/1221/upwelling_variable_jarvis_island_gove_thesis.pdf)), or an isopycnal. In the western equatorial Pacific the nitracline and chlorophyll maximum sit close to the thermocline at about 100 m ([Radenac et al. 2005](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00000/938/590.pdf)), and isotherm depth carries the ENSO signal directly. Isotherms do not work in the Red Sea, where the 20 °C isotherm does not exist in the upper ocean (background knowledge in the notes). N²- or salinity-based metrics are needed there.

The regional ecology argues for keeping Region × depth structure, and for one Red Sea tracer. The Red Sea DCM is permanent ([Wafar et al. 2021](https://pure.kfupm.edu.sa/en/publications/deep-chlorophyll-maximum-dcm-in-the-red-sea/)). In spring 2015 it deepened from about 20 m in the south to about 80 m in the north, close to Zeu. It rose to about 50 m in a cyclonic eddy, and surface chlorophyll was *lowest* in the central section although integrated stocks were similar (26.3-29.7 mg m⁻²) ([Kheireddine et al. 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2017.00132/full)). That last fact matters for your response. A ratio to surface is inflated wherever the denominator is smallest, so part of the Central Red Sea tail is plausibly a denominator effect rather than a bigger DCM. In September 2011, probably the same cruise as your data, the nutrient-rich Gulf of Aden Intermediate Water (low-salinity core at 50-65 m, nitrate > 10 µmol L⁻¹ near 75 m) ran along the Al-Lith banks at 17-18 °N ([Dreano et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5178994)). That depth is exactly mid-profile in your casts, and salinity at 50-75 m is a direct tracer of it. In the central Red Sea, low-salinity southern-origin patches carry more chlorophyll ([Zarokanellos et al. 2017](https://darchive.mblwhoilibrary.org/entities/publication/ed29560f-d995-5a64-9536-567d7f52a4b6/full)).

A parametric profile model makes the competing hypotheses explicit. The standard Gaussian-peak-on-background form describes 62.6% of more than 700 profiles in Mignot et al., against 31.8% sigmoid (mixed) ([Mignot et al. 2011](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/8/2391/2011/bg-8-2391-2011.pdf)). Uitz et al. give a dimensionless version on a linear background ([Uitz et al. 2006](https://people.mio.osupytheas.fr/~petrenko/TEACHING/OPCB343/Uitz_et_al._JGR_06_chloro_profiles_space.pdf)). Gaussian fits reproduce DCM depth and thickness well but intensity less well ([Gong et al. 2015](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/12/905/2015/bg-12-905-2015.pdf)). In brms each nonlinear parameter (peak depth zm, log amplitude, log width, background) takes its own linear predictor. Correlated cast-level effects use the `|ID|` syntax, and priors on every nonlinear parameter are "mandatory" ([Bürkner 2018](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.11123); [brms nonlinear vignette](https://paulbuerkner.com/brms/articles/brms_nonlinear.html)). Putting MLD into both zm and log-amplitude tests "shift" against "erode" directly. Cast-level random effects on the peak parameters are also the physically motivated answer to ρ ≈ 0.97, because most of that correlation is each cast's whole-shape departure from its regional mean. The notes found no published Bayesian or brms fit of this model, so it carries the highest convergence risk in the matrix.

Two measurement artefacts sit inside the response. Daytime non-photochemical quenching suppresses near-surface fluorescence. In 1,222 Southern Ocean profiles, uncorrected fluorescence underestimated surface Chl by about 18% on average and about 44% where quenching was significant ([Baldry et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2023.1302999/full)). Your 5-15 m denominator sits in that zone, so daytime casts inflate chl_norm at every depth below it. A 18-44% quench would inflate the ratio by about 1.2-1.8×, or +0.2 to +0.58 in log-ratio units (my arithmetic). Peak depth is robust to quenching while width and upper-profile shape are not ([Mignot et al. 2011](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/8/2391/2011/bg-8-2391-2011.pdf)), so shift-type comparisons are safer than amplitude-type ones. Carranza et al. sidestepped the problem by analysing night profiles only ([Carranza et al. 2018](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10082652)). No Red Sea or tropical-Pacific quenching magnitudes were found, so the effect has to be estimated from your casts.

**T1** isolates the binning change. It is supported if its date-blocked RMSE and per-region mean curves match the 1-m half-shift fit closely and its runtime drops about 5×. Set a tolerance in advance, for example DCM depth within 2-3 m. It is rejected if the peak curves flatten or shift, which would mean the bins are too coarse; retry at 2 m. **T2** follows from the confounding argument and Gelman's few-groups result. It is supported if unseen-date elpd and RMSE are unchanged or better and `sd(RegDate)` absorbs the ENSO variance. Compare `sample_prior = "yes"` draws against the posterior for the old `sd(enso)`, and look for a negative date-ENSO ridge in `pairs()`. It is rejected if a cruise-blocked stress test shows ENSO carried real out-of-cruise information. That would favour a *fixed* ENSO contrast restricted to the Equatorial region, or the z20 route in T11. **T3** fixes the impossible support and the heteroscedasticity. It is supported if Jacobian-corrected date-grouped elpd improves, 0% of predictive draws fall below −1, and the 90% interval coverage per date moves toward nominal. RMSE on the chl_norm scale, computed from the back-transformed mean, should be no worse. It is rejected if RMSE worsens materially because the mean back-transform mis-fits the Central Red Sea tail. In that case try Gamma(log) with an offset, which models the mean ratio directly, though this needs raw Chl (T13 data). **T4** runs only if the T3 posterior-predictive checks still show excess tail mass in specific regions.

**T5** tests Pedersen's core claim that shape information shared through a global smooth improves prediction for groups with little data ([Pedersen et al. 2019](https://peerj.com/articles/6876)). It is supported if unseen-date elpd improves or ties, with gains concentrated in Red Sea regions or other date-poor regions. It is rejected if the regional deviation `sds` stay large and unseen-date performance falls, meaning the regions really are distinct shapes. **T6** is the central MLD test. Its pooled-deviation design answers whether the regional MLD signal that wins on seen dates is real enough to survive shrinkage, and it replaces the failed regional tensors. It is supported if it beats T5 on unseen dates in the paired date bootstrap and the posterior keeps non-zero deviations only in regions where MLD varies widely across dates. It is rejected (keep the half-shift) if |Δelpd| < 4 or the bootstrap interval spans zero. **T7** follows the island-mass-effect evidence that enhancement spans the euphotic zone and varies by archipelago ([Gove et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4757766)). It is supported if the Region-specific distance slopes order roughly as NWHI/Hawaii strongest and Equatorial weakest, with a depth-varying profile, and unseen-date elpd improves. It is rejected if slopes pool to a common value and elpd is flat. Red Sea distance results need caution because shallow southern banks confound distance with latitude and cast depth ([Dreano et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5178994)). **T8** targets the ρ ≈ 0.97 problem. It is supported if residual lag-1 falls well below about 0.5 and per-date interval coverage becomes nominal, even if point RMSE barely changes; under dependence, honest intervals are the deliverable. It is rejected if φ is unidentifiable or trades off with the smooth `sds`, the failure mode Simpson documents ([Simpson 2018](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/322248v2.full)). In that case keep binning plus cast-level structure (T9). Whether `ar()` combines with distributional σ, threading, and the 5-m grid needs a `stancode()` check, because the notes could not verify it.

### Wave 3: the interpretable DCM model (T9-T10)

```r

f_T9 <- bf(lratio ~ b0 + slope * z + exp(logA) * exp(-0.5 * ((z - zm) / exp(logw))^2),

           logA  ~ 0 + Region + mld_z + ldist_z + (1 | c | cast),   # MLD "erodes" the DCM?

pri_T9 <- c(prior(normal(0, 0.5), nlpar = "b0"),   prior(normal(0, 1), nlpar = "slope"),

            prior(normal(0, 0.2), nlpar = "zm", coef = "mld_z"),
            prior(normal(-1.6, 0.5), nlpar = "logw"),
            prior(exponential(2), class = "sd", nlpar = "logA"),
            prior(exponential(5), class = "sd", nlpar = "zm"),
            prior(exponential(2), class = "sd", nlpar = "logw"),
            prior(lkj(2), class = "cor"))
# inits: zm per Region from Phase-0 per-cast peaks (Red Sea South ~0.2-0.5; Pacific ~1.0);
#   control = list(adapt_delta = 0.95, max_treedepth = 12); pilot on 100 casts with pathfinder first

# T10 (optional): smooth background + parametric peak; keep the background smooth stiff
#   lratio ~ bg + exp(logA) * exp(-0.5 * ((z - zm) / exp(logw))^2),
#   bg ~ 0 + Region + s(z, by = Region, k = 4), other nlpars as in T9
```

**T9** turns the shift-vs-erosion question into two coefficients with ecological units. The zm coefficient on mld_z is the DCM displacement per SD of MLD, directly comparable with the 0.5 the half-shift imposes. A negative logA coefficient means MLD dilutes or erodes the peak, as the Zeu/MLD switch literature predicts ([Uitz et al. 2006](https://people.mio.osupytheas.fr/~petrenko/TEACHING/OPCB343/Uitz_et_al._JGR_06_chloro_profiles_space.pdf); [Cornec et al. 2021](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00685/79669/82442.pdf)). The correlated cast effects stand in for the whole-profile deviations behind ρ ≈ 0.97. The DCM-depth sub-model is also the most quenching-robust quantity ([Mignot et al. 2011](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/8/2391/2011/bg-8-2391-2011.pdf)). The model is supported if it converges (R̂ < 1.01, no multimodality across chains) and matches or beats the best GAM on unseen-date elpd. Even a tie is a win given the interpretability. It is rejected if chains settle in different modes, typically because casts with no DCM leave zm and logw unidentified, or if it loses clearly. In that case use the Cornec flag to fit it only on DCM-present casts, or move to a peak-vs-no-peak mixture. **T10** adds a stiff smooth background so the peak cannot be absorbed. Run it only if T9 converges but shows systematic background misfit. The documented route places `s()` inside a nonlinear parameter's formula, and the notes found no published brms example of it.

**T11** is the best-backed ecological test in the set. DCM depth tracks the nitracline and isolumes, and in the tropical Pacific the thermocline is the nitracline proxy that also carries ENSO ([Cullen 2015](https://ftp.soest.hawaii.edu/dkarl/misc/dave/Oxford/09-Plankton%20Growth%20and%20Metabolism%20in%20the%20Epipelagic%20Zone/2015AnnuRevMarSci7-207-239-Cullen.pdf); [Radenac et al. 2005](https://archimer.ifremer.fr/doc/00000/938/590.pdf); [Gove 2005](https://data.nodc.noaa.gov/coris/library/NOAA/CRCP/project/1221/upwelling_variable_jarvis_island_gove_thesis.pdf)). It is supported if it beats the half-shift on unseen dates *and* the regional deviation `sds` shrink, meaning the new coordinate collapses regional shapes toward one curve. It is rejected if MLD-shifted depth still wins. That outcome would mean MLD captures something beyond pycnocline position, such as surface-layer dilution of the denominator. A density-criterion MLD (0.03 and 0.005 kg m⁻³) should be tried in the Phase-0 screen alongside, following Carranza et al.'s finding ([Carranza et al. 2018](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10082652)). **T12** is supported if the night term is negative in the upper profile and of order −0.2 to −0.6 log units (the 18-44% quench range from [Baldry et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2023.1302999/full)), and if the night-only refit changes enhancement magnitudes but not DCM depth. It is rejected if the term is near zero, in which case quenching is negligible for these casts and day and night can be pooled. **T13** tests the assumption built into chl_norm that deep Chl scales one-for-one with surface Chl. A depth-varying slope below 1 at the DCM means low-surface casts get inflated ratios, the suspected Central Red Sea mechanism ([Kheireddine et al. 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2017.00132/full)). Mignot et al. found surface Chl the strongest single correlate of DCM depth (r² = 0.81), which also justifies it as a predictor ([Mignot et al. 2011](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/8/2391/2011/bg-8-2391-2011.pdf)). The test is supported if the slope departs from 1 below the surface layer and Central Red Sea residuals shrink. It is rejected if the slope stays near 1 everywhere. A noisy surface estimate attenuates that slope toward zero, so averaging surface over the full 5-15 m layer, or a measurement-error term, matters here. **T14** is supported if a lower-salinity (GAIW-influenced) signal predicts a shallower or stronger subsurface maximum in South/Central casts, as the Sep 2011 hydrography suggests ([Dreano et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5178994)). It is rejected if the term is flat after Region.

### Validation protocol: date-grouped first, conditional last

Use **one frozen date-grouped holdout for every test**. Create it once with `loo::kfold_split_grouped` on `RegDate`, stratified so each Region keeps training dates, and keep your existing unseen-date set if possible so results compare with the 1.540 baseline. Fit each candidate once on the training dates and score the held-out dates with `allow_new_levels = TRUE, sample_new_levels = "gaussian"` ([brms kfold reference](https://paulbuerkner.com/brms/reference/kfold.brmsfit.html)). The primary score is the **joint log predictive density per held-out date** on the chl_norm scale, Jacobian-corrected for log-scale models. This mirrors `joint = "group"`, which Vehtari describes as the right target for predicting a new group "accounting for within-group dependence" ([Stan Discourse](https://discourse.mc-stan.org/t/difference-between-kfold-joint-group-and-kfold-joint-false-for-brms-model/39081)). The log score is the recommended general criterion, alongside an application loss ([CV FAQ](https://users.aalto.fi/~ave/CV-FAQ.html)). Report three secondary scores. The first is RMSE on the chl_norm scale from marginal predictions (`re_formula = NA` and new-level draws), plus RMSE on the log scale, so a few Central Red Sea tails do not decide everything. The second is per-cast DCM-depth and peak-enhancement error as the ecological loss. The third is 90% interval coverage per date. Compare models through **paired per-date differences with a 4,000-draw cluster bootstrap over dates**, or over cruises where a cruise spans regions, broken down by Region. Prefer the simpler or more pooled model when |Δelpd| < 4 or the interval spans zero.

Validate the 2-3 finalists with full 10-fold date-grouped `kfold()` on binned data (`chains = 2`, `Ksub` first if needed). Derive stacking weights from that date-blocked pointwise elpd, never from PSIS-LOO, since stacking is built for the M-open case ([Yao et al. 2018](https://projecteuclid.org/euclid.ba/1516093227); [loo weights vignette](https://mc-stan.org/loo/articles/loo2-weights.html)). Run two stress tests: leave-one-cruise-out, and leave-one-ENSO-phase-out in the Pacific. Treat these as extrapolation checks, not the primary criterion. Leave-future-out CV applies only if forecasting later cruises is the stated use ([Bürkner, Gabry & Vehtari 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06281)). Keep reporting cast-holdout results, labelled explicitly as the conditional (known-date) estimand.

## Conclusion

The evidence reframes the project. The question is no longer which regional MLD term fits best. It is which depth anchor and how much regional pooling generalize to a cruise the model has never seen, and those are separable questions that the matrix tests one at a time. The single most consequential idea is that MLD has probably been acting as a noisy stand-in for pycnocline depth and, through the 5-15 m denominator, for surface-layer conditions. If T11 and T13 can be run, they decide whether MLD stays in the model at all. Without the extra data, T6 (pooled MLD on the half-shift) and T9 (shift vs erosion in a peak model) give the best available answer.

The practical implication is that the cost problem largely dissolves before any science changes. Binning plus a log-ratio likelihood turns 20-80 h fits into roughly 4-16 h fits, makes negative-chlorophyll predictions impossible, and makes intervals more honest. That in turn makes proper date-grouped K-fold, and stacking of the survivors, affordable for the finalists. Two cautions remain: none of the code was executed, and the cost scaling, `ar()` and threading compatibility, and nonlinear-model convergence all need short pilot runs before multi-day fits.
